Research findings

Key data from the May 2022 GPS field survey: 95 sections covering 10.67 km of GPS-walked length (13.16 km AutoCAD-refined main alignment).

The central hydraulic finding
Actual bed slope vs Lacey self-cleansing slope:
S_actual = 0.01529 (1 in 65)
S_Lacey = 0.000440 (1 in 2,275)
The canal is 34.8× steeper than needed for self-cleansing flow.
Silting is not a design failure — it is caused entirely by intake blockage, encroachment, and waste dumping. The canal's hydraulic design is sound.

Key findings

🏛️
Licchavi inscriptions name the canal 'Tilmak' — a non-Sanskritic word anomalous in Licchavi-era records. Pre-Licchavi (Kirat) origin is one of three working hypotheses; resolution requires further epigraphic and archaeological work.
💧
Perennial clean flow is limited to the upper 3,432 m. Beyond chainage 3+124 m, flow becomes contaminated by sewage inflow.
⚙️
The canal's slope is 34.8× steeper than Lacey's self-cleansing threshold. The design is hydraulically sound — deterioration is entirely human-caused.
🚧
6 sewage inflow points identified at chainages 0+524 m, 3+254 m, 3+384 m, 4+973 m, 6+220 m, and 9+412 m.
🗺️
Roads cover 3,183 m of the canal alignment. The 5–7 km band is the most critical zone for restoration intervention.
